CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY

1. Short title and commencement

(1) These rules may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.

(2) They shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification, appoint.

2. Definitions

In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Act" means the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023;

(b) "consent artefact" means a machine-readable electronic record generated each time a Data Principal gives, manages, reviews or withdraws her consent;

(c) "verifiable parental consent" means a mechanism to verify the identity and age of a parent prior to the parent giving consent for processing of personal data of a child.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER II — NOTICE

3. Notice to Data Principal

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall give notice to a Data Principal in accordance with section 5 of the Act, and such notice shall—
    (a) specify the personal data sought to be collected and the purpose of processing;
    (b) specify the manner in which the Data Principal may exercise her rights under the Act;
    (c) specify the manner in which a complaint may be made to the Board;
    (d) be provided before requesting consent; and
    (e) be made available in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India.

(2) A Data Fiduciary shall not—
    (a) obtain consent through deceptive design patterns;
    (b) bundle consent requests for different purposes; or
    (c) make consent a precondition for services where such consent is not necessary for the service.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER III — CONSENT

4. Consent

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall implement a mechanism for a Data Principal to give, manage, review and withdraw her consent.

(2) The consent mechanism shall—
    (a) enable a Data Principal to withdraw consent as easily as she gave it;
    (b) not use dark patterns or deceptive design to discourage withdrawal; and
    (c) ensure that withdrawal of consent does not affect the lawfulness of prior processing.

(3) Each consent request shall be accompanied by the relevant notice and shall not be bundled with other terms and conditions.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER IV — SECURITY SAFEGUARDS

5. Security safeguards

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure reasonable security safeguards to protect personal data.

(2) Such measures shall include, as applicable:—
    (a) use of encryption for personal data;
    (b) control over access to personal data;
    (c) measures to ensure ongoing confidentiality, integrity and availability of personal data and processing systems and services;
    (d) measures to restore the availability and access to personal data in the event of a physical or technical incident in a timely manner;
    (e) regular testing and evaluation of the effectiveness of security measures.

6. Breach notification

(1) In the event of a personal data breach, a Data Fiduciary shall notify the Board as soon as possible, and in any event not later than seventy-two hours after becoming aware of the breach.

(2) The notification shall include, to the extent possible:—
    (a) the nature of the breach;
    (b) the categories and approximate number of Data Principals affected;
    (c) the categories and approximate amount of personal data records affected;
    (d) the likely consequences of the breach;
    (e) measures taken or proposed to address the breach.

(3) Each affected Data Principal shall also be notified of the breach in a timely manner.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER V — DATA RETENTION AND ERASURE

7. Retention and erasure of personal data

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall retain personal data only for as long as is necessary to fulfil the purpose for which it was collected, unless retention is required by any other law.

(2) Upon fulfillment of the purpose or withdrawal of consent (where applicable), the Data Fiduciary shall—
    (a) erase the personal data; and
    (b) cause the Data Processor to erase personal data held by it.

(3) Data Fiduciaries shall implement mechanisms to ensure timely and systematic erasure of personal data, including from backup systems.

(4) Where a Data Principal requests erasure, the Data Fiduciary shall comply within the period specified by the Board.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER VI — DATA PRINCIPAL RIGHTS

8. Right to information

(1) A Data Principal may request a Data Fiduciary to provide the information referred to in section 11 of the Act.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall respond within thirty days of receipt of the request.

9. Right to correction, completion and erasure

(1) A Data Principal may request correction, completion or erasure of personal data.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall take action within thirty days of receipt of the request.

10. Grievance redressal

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall designate a Grievance Officer.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall—
    (a) publish the name, contact details, and jurisdiction of the Grievance Officer on its website in a readily accessible manner;
    (b) establish an effective grievance redressal mechanism; and
    (c) acknowledge grievance complaints within forty-eight hours and resolve them within thirty days.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER VII — DATA PROCESSORS

11. Obligations relating to Data Processors

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall—
    (a) enter into a written contract with each Data Processor prior to engaging the Data Processor to process personal data;
    (b) ensure the contract requires the Data Processor to process personal data only in accordance with the instructions of the Data Fiduciary;
    (c) ensure the contract imposes security obligations on the Data Processor; and
    (d) require the Data Processor to notify the Data Fiduciary of any personal data breach.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall maintain a register of all Data Processors.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER VIII — SIGNIFICANT DATA FIDUCIARIES

12. Significant Data Fiduciaries

(1) The Central Government may, having regard to the volume and sensitivity of personal data processed, the risk to the rights of Data Principals, the potential impact on the sovereignty and integrity of India, and the risk to national security and public order, notify any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as Significant Data Fiduciaries.

(2) Significant Data Fiduciaries shall, in addition to obligations under the Act, comply with additional obligations including:—
    (a) appoint a Data Protection Officer resident in India;
    (b) appoint an independent Data Auditor;
    (c) undertake periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments; and
    (d) undertake annual audits by a Data Auditor.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CHAPTER IX — CHILDREN'S DATA

13. Processing of children's personal data

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall, before processing personal data of a child, obtain verifiable consent of the parent in the prescribed manner.

(2) A Data Fiduciary shall not:—
    (a) process personal data of a child in a manner likely to cause detrimental effect on the well-being of the child;
    (b) track or monitor the behaviour of children;
    (c) target advertising directed at children; or
    (d) profile children for any purpose.

(3) Age verification shall be implemented using reliable mechanisms prescribed by the Board.
