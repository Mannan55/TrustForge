CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY

1. Short title, extent, commencement and application

(1) This Act may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

(2) It extends to the whole of India and also applies to processing of digital personal data outside the territory of India, if such processing is in connection with any profiling of, or activity of offering goods or services to Data Principals within the territory of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, and different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act.

2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Consent Manager" means a person registered with the Board, who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review and withdraw her consent through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform;

(b) "Data" means a representation of information, facts, concepts, opinions or instructions in a manner suitable for communication, interpretation or processing by human beings or by automated means;

(c) "Data Auditor" means an independent data auditor referred to in section 29;

(d) "Data Fiduciary" means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data;

(e) "Data Principal" means the individual to whom the personal data relates and where such individual is—
    (i) a child, includes the parents or lawful guardian of such a child;
    (ii) a person with disability, includes her lawful guardian, acting on her behalf;

(f) "Data Processor" means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary;

(g) "personal data" means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data;

(h) "processing" means a wholly or partly automated operation or set of operations performed on digital personal data, and includes operations such as collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, alignment or combination, indexing, sharing, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, restriction, erasure or destruction;

(i) "Significant Data Fiduciary" means any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as may be notified as such by the Central Government under section 10;

CHAPTER II — GROUNDS FOR PROCESSING PERSONAL DATA

3. Grounds for processing personal data

A person may process the personal data of a Data Principal only in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

4. Processing of personal data and purposes

(1) A person may process personal data of a Data Principal only for a lawful purpose—
    (a) for which the Data Principal has given her consent; or
    (b) for certain legitimate uses.

(2) For the purposes of this section, a "lawful purpose" means any purpose which is not expressly forbidden by law.

5. Notice

(1) Where personal data is processed on the basis of consent, every Data Fiduciary shall give to each Data Principal, before requesting for her consent, a notice containing—
    (a) a description of personal data sought to be collected and the purpose of processing of such personal data;
    (b) the manner in which she may exercise her rights under the provisions of this Act; and
    (c) the manner in which the Data Principal may make a complaint to the Board.

(2) Where a Data Fiduciary had been collecting personal data prior to the commencement of this Act, such Data Fiduciary shall, as soon as it is reasonably practicable, give to each such Data Principal a notice referred to in sub-section (1).

(3) The notice referred to in sub-section (1) shall be—
    (a) made available in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution; and
    (b) itemised in a clear and plain language.

6. Consent

(1) Consent of the Data Principal shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action, and shall signify an agreement to the processing of her personal data for the specified purpose and be limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose.

(2) The consent given by the Data Principal shall be accompanied by the notice referred to in section 5, which shall be in clear and plain language.

(3) The consent given by the Data Principal may be withdrawn by her at any time, by sending a notice to the Data Fiduciary as per the procedure referred to in section 12(3).

(4) The withdrawal of consent shall be as easy as giving consent.

(5) The consequences of withdrawal of consent shall be borne by the Data Principal.

(6) The withdrawal of consent shall not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal.

(7) Every request made to a Data Principal for consent shall be presented in a manner that—
    (a) is in clear and plain language;
    (b) specifies the personal data sought to be collected and the purpose of processing;
    (c) provides contact details of a Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or of any other person authorised by the Data Fiduciary to respond to any communication from the Data Principal for the purpose of exercise of her rights under the provisions of this Act; and
    (d) contains a list of all Data Fiduciaries with whom the personal data may be shared.

CHAPTER III — RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF DATA PRINCIPAL

10. Rights and duties of Data Principal

(1) Every Data Principal shall have the following rights, namely:—
    (a) the right to obtain from the Data Fiduciary—
        (i) a summary of personal data being processed and the processing activities undertaken by that Data Fiduciary with respect to such personal data;
        (ii) the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom the personal data has been shared by that Data Fiduciary, along with a description of the personal data so shared; and
        (iii) any other information related to the personal data of such Data Principal and its processing, as may be prescribed;
    (b) the right to correction and erasure; and
    (c) the right of grievance redressal.

11. Right to access information about personal data

(1) A Data Principal shall have the right to obtain from the Data Fiduciary—
    (a) a summary of personal data being processed and the processing activities undertaken by that Data Fiduciary with respect to such personal data;
    (b) the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom the personal data has been shared by that Data Fiduciary, along with a description of the personal data so shared; and
    (c) any other information related to the personal data of such Data Principal and its processing, as may be prescribed.

(2) The right under sub-section (1) shall not apply to personal data of a Data Principal or any other person.

12. Right to correction, completion, updating and erasure of personal data

(1) A Data Principal shall have the right to—
    (a) correction of inaccurate or misleading personal data;
    (b) completion of incomplete personal data;
    (c) updating of personal data; and
    (d) erasure of personal data, which is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected or processed.

(2) Upon receiving a request from the Data Principal, the Data Fiduciary shall correct the inaccurate or misleading personal data, complete the incomplete personal data, update the personal data, and in the manner and within the period specified by the Board, erase the personal data that is no longer necessary for the specified purpose.

13. Right of grievance redressal

(1) A Data Principal shall have the right to have readily available means of grievance redressal provided by a Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager in respect of any act or omission of such Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager regarding the performance of its obligations in relation to the personal data of such Data Principal or the exercise of her rights under the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall respond to each grievance referred to in sub-section (1) within such period as may be prescribed.

14. Right to nominate

A Data Principal shall have the right to nominate, in such manner as may be prescribed, any other individual, who shall, in the event of death or incapacity of the Data Principal, exercise the rights of the Data Principal in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

CHAPTER IV — OBLIGATIONS OF DATA FIDUCIARY

15. General obligations of Data Fiduciary

(1) Each Data Fiduciary shall—
    (a) process personal data only for a specified purpose for which—
        (i) the Data Principal has given her consent; or
        (ii) for certain legitimate uses under section 7;
    (b) ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency of personal data that is likely to be used to make a decision that affects the Data Principal or disclosed to another Data Fiduciary;
    (c) implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure effective observance of the provisions of this Act and the rules made thereunder and safeguard personal data.

16. Security safeguards

(1) Each Data Fiduciary shall protect personal data in its possession or under its control by taking reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach.

(2) In the event of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary shall—
    (a) give the Board and each affected Data Principal notice of such breach in such manner and within such period as may be prescribed; and
    (b) take such remedial measures as may be prescribed or as directed by the Board.

17. Retention of personal data

(1) The Data Fiduciary shall not retain personal data beyond the period necessary to fulfil the purpose for which it has been collected.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall, at the end of the specified period or after the purpose is no longer served, delete the personal data and cause the Data Processor, if any, to delete the personal data held by it, unless its retention is required for compliance with any other law currently in force.

18. Grievance redressal

(1) Every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall—
    (a) establish an effective mechanism to redress grievances of Data Principals; and
    (b) be required to designate a Grievance Officer and publish the name and contact details of such Officer on its website, and respond to each grievance in the manner prescribed and within the period prescribed.

19. Processing of personal data of children

(1) The Data Fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child—
    (a) obtain verifiable consent of the parent of such child or the lawful guardian, as the case may be, in such manner as may be prescribed; and
    (b) not undertake processing of personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child.

(2) The Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children.

CHAPTER IV-A — OBLIGATIONS IN RELATION TO DATA PROCESSORS

20. Obligations in relation to Data Processors

(1) A Data Fiduciary shall enter into a valid contract with a Data Processor pursuant to which the Data Processor shall process the personal data only in accordance with the instructions of the Data Fiduciary and in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

(2) The contract shall impose obligations on the Data Processor to—
    (a) process personal data only in the manner, to the extent and for the purposes specified in the contract; and
    (b) implement appropriate security safeguards in compliance with the provisions of this Act.

(3) The Data Fiduciary shall remain responsible for the processing of personal data by the Data Processor in compliance with the provisions of this Act.

CHAPTER VI — DATA PROTECTION BOARD OF INDIA

28. Establishment of Data Protection Board of India

(1) The Central Government shall, by notification, establish a Board to be known as the Data Protection Board of India.

(2) The Board shall be a body corporate, having perpetual succession and a common seal, with the power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, both movable and immovable, and to contract and shall, by the said name, sue or be sued.

CHAPTER VII — PENALTIES

31. Penalties

(1) The Board may, after conducting an inquiry in such manner as may be prescribed, impose such financial penalty as may be specified in Schedule I, on a Data Fiduciary for breach of the provisions of this Act.

SCHEDULE I — Financial Penalties

The following penalties are specified for breach:

1. Failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach (Section 8(5)): Financial penalty of up to rupees two hundred and fifty crore (₹250 Crore).

2. Failure to give notice to the Board and affected individuals of a personal data breach: Financial penalty of up to rupees two hundred crore (₹200 Crore).

3. Non-fulfilment of additional obligations in relation to children: Financial penalty of up to rupees two hundred crore (₹200 Crore).

4. Non-fulfilment of additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciaries: Financial penalty of up to rupees one hundred and fifty crore (₹150 Crore).

5. Failure to comply with Board orders: Financial penalty of up to rupees ten thousand crore (₹10,000 Crore).

6. Breach of any other provision: Financial penalty of up to rupees fifty crore (₹50 Crore).
